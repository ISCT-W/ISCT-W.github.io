PaRaMS: Preserving Model Utility While Disrupting Direct Merging

Model merging can combine the capabilities of fine-tuned models through parameter operations without retraining.

WJunhao WeiAbout 6 min readICCV 2025 · Published

Model merging can combine the capabilities of fine-tuned models through parameter operations without retraining. For developers who invested in data and computation, this raises a question: can a model remain available for use while making unauthorized, low-cost inheritance of its capabilities through merging harder?

Our ICCV 2025 paper, Disrupting Model Merging: A Parameter-Level Defense Without Sacrificing Accuracy, introduces PaRaMS. I am a co-first author and the first-listed author. The method uses parameter symmetries to separate standalone usability from compatibility with direct parameter merging.

One function can have different parameter representations

Let θdef\theta_{\mathrm{def}}θdef​ be the defender's model and θfr\theta_{\mathrm{fr}}θfr​ another model fine-tuned from the same pretrained base. The defender releases θ^def=η(θdef)\hat\theta_{\mathrm{def}}=\eta(\theta_{\mathrm{def}})θ^def​=η(θdef​), seeking to preserve:

f(x;θ^def)=f(x;θdef),f(x;\hat\theta_{\mathrm{def}})=f(x;\theta_{\mathrm{def}}),f(x;θ^def​)=f(x;θdef​),

Meanwhile, under the specified merging operation M\mathcal MM, the merged model should perform substantially worse on the defender's task. The first requirement preserves standalone use; the second targets direct inheritance through merging.

The reuser can read public weights and holds a model from the same base, while the defender cannot inspect that specific model beforehand. The study focuses on resource-limited reusers relying on parameter operations, rather than parties with arbitrary retraining budgets. It evaluates a particular reuse path.

There is no contradiction: hidden-unit permutations and paired rescalings can change parameters without changing the function. Same-base fine-tuned models often have parameter correspondences suitable for interpolation. PaRaMS disrupts those correspondences through function-preserving transformations.

Original Figure 2, PDF page 4 (proceedings page 17701). The protected model remains usable, but direct interpolation with another model may cross high-loss regions. This is a conceptual illustration, not a full measured reconstruction of the loss landscape.

First transformation: renumber hidden units

Consider a two-layer MLP:

MLP⁡(X)=W2 σ(W1X+b1)+b2.\operatorname{MLP}(X)=W_2\,\sigma(W_1X+b_1)+b_2.MLP(X)=W2​σ(W1​X+b1​)+b2​.

For permutation matrix PPP, transform both layers:

W1′=PW1,b1′=Pb1,W2′=W2P⊤.W'_1=PW_1,\qquad b'_1=Pb_1,\qquad W'_2=W_2P^\top.W1′​=PW1​,b1′​=Pb1​,W2′​=W2​P⊤.

An elementwise activation satisfies σ(Pz)=Pσ(z)\sigma(Pz)=P\sigma(z)σ(Pz)=Pσ(z), and P⊤P=IP^\top P=IP⊤P=I. The second layer cancels the first layer's permutation, preserving standalone output. Merging with an unpermuted model can nevertheless mix units that represent different things.

A unit moved from position one to position three is still found by the next layer, but position three in another network may have a different role. Coordinate-wise averaging then combines incompatible numbering. Unlike arbitrary noise, paired transformations offer an algebraic reason for output preservation.

PaRaMS selects permutations that move MLP parameters away from the pretrained parameters, formulating the search as a linear assignment problem. It changes parameter representation rather than training the model to forget its task. Method, page 4

Second transformation: paired scaling inside attention

For the paper's attention expression:

Attention⁡(Q,K,V)=softmax⁡ ⁣(QK⊤d)VWO,\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V) =\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)VW_O,Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(d​QK⊤​)VWO​,

Choose invertible diagonal matrices A,BA,BA,B and set:

Q′=QA,K′=KA−1,V′=VB,WO′=B−1WO.Q'=QA,\quad K'=KA^{-1},\quad V'=VB,\quad W'_O=B^{-1}W_O.Q′=QA,K′=KA−1,V′=VB,WO′​=B−1WO​.

Since diagonal matrices equal their transposes, Q′K′⊤=QK⊤Q'K'^\top=QK^\topQ′K′⊤=QK⊤ and V′WO′=VWOV'W'_O=VW_OV′WO′​=VWO​. Attention scores and outputs remain unchanged. The method applies these rescalings within multi-head attention and combines them with MLP permutations. Derivation, page 5

Equivalence holds for the defined modules in exact arithmetic. Operator connections, floating-point effects, and implementation details still require verification for each architecture.

Larger parameter distance guides the construction, but does not prove that every merging method must fail. Parameter space has other symmetries and paths, and some algorithms realign models. Algebraic function preservation and experimentally reduced merging performance are distinct claims.

Evaluate standalone and merged performance together

The paper distinguishes unmerged/unprotected (UMP−), unmerged/protected (UMP+), merged/unprotected (MMP−), and merged/protected (MMP+). The objective is met when UMP+ retains utility while MMP+ is substantially worse than MMP−.

Original Figure 3, PDF page 7 (proceedings page 17704). Groups compare TA, TIES, weight averaging, and AdaMerging, with and without DARE. The upper baselines show standalone models before and after protection; protected merged models have markedly lower bars.

Classification covers three CLIP-pretrained ViTs and seven datasets. In one ViT-B-32 Task Arithmetic configuration, protecting Cars reduces merged accuracy on Cars / RESISC45 from 70.29% / 94.24% to 0.51% / 2.13%. These are results for that configuration, not a universal reduction. Table 1

With Stable Diffusion 1.5, protected standalone models still generate clear images, while the displayed merged examples become noisy. Mean image–text alignment scores for two prompt groups fall from 0.3416 and 0.3386 to 0.1277 and 0.0820. CLIP alignment measures representation-level consistency, not a complete human quality assessment. Table 2

Original Figure 4, PDF page 7 (proceedings page 17704). Columns are UMP−, UMP+, the other model, MMP−, and MMP+. The first two illustrate standalone utility, the last merging disruption. Different generated samples do not demonstrate pixelwise equality.

Llama2 text-classification experiments use Emotion and Twitter. Full-parameter merged accuracy falls from 97.6% and 95.3% to 21.4% and 35.8%; scaling only parameter-efficient modules has a weaker effect. This suggests cross-modal applicability, but text classification is not a comprehensive evaluation of open-ended LLM generation. Table 3

The key boundary: recovery by an informed reuser

An informed party can attempt to realign protected parameters with the public base. In the adaptive experiment, merged accuracy is 84.13% without protection and 7.12% with standard PaRaMS, but recovers to 80.32% after adaptation. The basic method is therefore not irreversible capability access control.

Additional random pruning reduces an example standalone accuracy from 97.46% to 96.33%, while lowering adaptively recovered merged accuracy to 64.35%. This introduces a utility cost and loses strict functional equivalence. Distillation bypasses parameter correspondence and recovers 82.27% in that experiment, requiring extra data and training. Whether that cost deters reuse depends on resources and incentives. Page 8, Table 4

Preserving a function does not preserve its behavior under parameter composition. PaRaMS raises the difficulty of direct merging within a defined threat model; it does not block every path for transferring capabilities.
