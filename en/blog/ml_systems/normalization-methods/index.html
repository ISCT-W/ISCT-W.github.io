Normalization: Statistical Axes, Residual Placement, and Deployment

Similar normalization names can hide entirely different statistical axes.

WJunhao WeiAbout 9 min read

Similar normalization names can hide entirely different statistical axes. First identify which elements share statistics and whether inference uses current or running estimates. Separately determine whether normalization sits before or after a residual sublayer. Type and placement answer different questions.

Statistical groups determine which scale differences disappear; residual placement determines gradient paths. Both must match tensor shapes and execution conditions.

A shared formula and statistical sets

For a set SSS of mmm scalars, centered variance normalization is:

μS=1m∑j∈Sxj,σS2=1m∑j∈S(xj−μS)2,\mu_S=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j\in S}x_j, \qquad \sigma_S^2=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j\in S}(x_j-\mu_S)^2,μS​=m1​j∈S∑​xj​,σS2​=m1​j∈S∑​(xj​−μS​)2, x^i=xi−μSσS2+ε,yi=γix^i+βi.\hat{x}_i=\frac{x_i-\mu_S}{\sqrt{\sigma_S^2+\varepsilon}}, \qquad y_i=\gamma_i\hat{x}_i+\beta_i.x^i​=σS2​+ε​xi​−μS​​,yi​=γi​x^i​+βi​.

ε\varepsilonε protects against very small variance; γ,β\gamma,\betaγ,β are learned scale and shift. Methods differ mainly in SSS and the source of inference statistics.

Norm, normalization, and standardization

“Norm” in BN/LN means normalization, not a vector norm. Statistics texts sometimes reserve normalization for range scaling and standardization for zero mean/unit variance. Established deep-learning names include centering, scaling, and learned affine transforms. Specify equations and axes rather than relying on terminology.

“Reducing internal covariate shift” is a historical explanation. A more cautious engineering account is control of activation scales and improved optimization conditions, reducing sensitivity to initialization and learning rates—not guaranteeing against vanishing or exploding gradients.

The axes at a glance

For CNN X∈RN×C×H×WX\in\mathbb R^{N\times C\times H\times W}X∈RN×C×H×W and Transformer X∈RB×T×DX\in\mathbb R^{B\times T\times D}X∈RB×T×D:

BN depends on other samples; LN, RMSNorm, IN, and GN can operate independently per sample.

Share statistics only where comparison makes sense

BN resembles comparing one school subject across students; LN resembles adjusting one student's overall scale across subjects. The former assumes reasonably comparable population statistics; the latter normalizes one representation without other samples.

Choosing axes decides which means and scales can be removed. If absolute intensity carries information, validate before normalizing it away. Learned affine parameters restore general scale/shift capacity, not each sample's discarded statistics. Hidden channels are not literally school subjects: Transformer LN normally computes along DDD separately for each (b,t)(b,t)(b,t), not across whole sequences or batches.

BatchNorm: channel statistics across samples

Formula and axes

For CNN tensors, each channel has:

μc=1NHW∑n,h,wxnchw,\mu_c=\frac{1}{NHW}\sum_{n,h,w}x_{nchw},μc​=NHW1​n,h,w∑​xnchw​, σc2=1NHW∑n,h,w(xnchw−μc)2,\sigma_c^2=\frac{1}{NHW}\sum_{n,h,w}(x_{nchw}-\mu_c)^2,σc2​=NHW1​n,h,w∑​(xnchw​−μc​)2, ynchw=γcxnchw−μcσc2+ε+βc.y_{nchw}=\gamma_c \frac{x_{nchw}-\mu_c}{\sqrt{\sigma_c^2+\varepsilon}} +\beta_c.ynchw​=γc​σc2​+ε​xnchw​−μc​​+βc​.

Fully connected BN aggregates each feature across the batch; CNN BN also includes spatial positions.

Training versus inference

Training uses minibatch statistics and updates running mean/variance; inference usually uses those running estimates. Forgetting eval() changes behavior. Gradient accumulation enlarges an optimizer update's effective batch, not the microbatch visible to BN.

Benefits

With sufficiently large, approximately independent batches, BN stabilizes channel scales, works well in many classic CNNs, and can introduce useful stochastic regularization. Fixed inference statistics permit folding into a preceding convolution/linear layer:

z=Wx+b,y=γz−μσ2+ε+β,z=Wx+b, \qquad y=\gamma\frac{z-\mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\varepsilon}}+\beta,z=Wx+b,y=γσ2+ε​z−μ​+β,

The folded parameters are:

W′=γσ2+εW,b′=γ(b−μ)σ2+ε+β.W'=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\varepsilon}}W, \qquad b'=\frac{\gamma(b-\mu)}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\varepsilon}}+\beta.W′=σ2+ε​γ​W,b′=σ2+ε​γ(b−μ)​+β.

Common problems

Small batches give noisy estimates; ordinary distributed BN uses device-local statistics, while SyncBN adds communication. Domain shifts can invalidate running estimates. Padding can contaminate sequence statistics, and correlated examples such as adjacent video frames can skew them.

Sampling noise and small batches

A sample's normalized output changes with its batch companions, introducing noise, but BN is not a complete dropout replacement. Small per-device batches—common in high-resolution vision—make both instantaneous and running estimates less stable.

LayerNorm: normalize each representation independently

Formula and axes

For Transformer X∈RB×T×DX\in\mathbb R^{B\times T\times D}X∈RB×T×D, normalize hidden features per token:

μbt=1D∑d=1Dxbtd,\mu_{bt}=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}x_{btd},μbt​=D1​d=1∑D​xbtd​, σbt2=1D∑d=1D(xbtd−μbt)2,\sigma_{bt}^2=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}(x_{btd}-\mu_{bt})^2,σbt2​=D1​d=1∑D​(xbtd​−μbt​)2, ybtd=γdxbtd−μbtσbt2+ε+βd.y_{btd}=\gamma_d \frac{x_{btd}-\mu_{bt}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{bt}^2+\varepsilon}} +\beta_d.ybtd​=γd​σbt2​+ε​xbtd​−μbt​​+βd​.

Why it suits Transformers

BN across samples/tokens depends on batch composition, length, padding, and position distributions. LN instead uses one token's features. Training and inference share the same current-input calculation, supporting variable lengths, small batches, accumulation, and token-wise decoding without running estimates or cross-sample synchronization.

Cautions

normalized_shape selects trailing axes; LN does not always mean “channels.” It removes mean and rescales variance. It still costs computation and memory traffic, making fusion useful. Mixed-precision implementations often accumulate statistics in FP32 before casting back.

Consistent statistics in autoregressive execution

LN needs no training-population estimates. BN is mathematically possible in sequence models, but lengths, padding, small batches, and train/inference differences complicate it. Token-local LN/RMSNorm better match typical decoding conditions.

RMSNorm: scale by RMS without centering

Often using the same feature axes as LN, RMSNorm computes:

RMS⁡(x)=1D∑d=1Dxd2+ε,\operatorname{RMS}(x)= \sqrt{\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}x_d^2+\varepsilon},RMS(x)=D1​d=1∑D​xd2​+ε​, yd=γdxdRMS⁡(x).y_d=\gamma_d\frac{x_d}{\operatorname{RMS}(x)}.yd​=γd​RMS(x)xd​​.

Bias support varies by implementation.

InstanceNorm and GroupNorm: spatial statistics within samples

Instance normalization

For each sample and channel, aggregate only over H,WH,WH,W:

μnc=1HW∑h,wxnchw,σnc2=1HW∑h,w(xnchw−μnc)2.\mu_{nc}=\frac{1}{HW}\sum_{h,w}x_{nchw}, \qquad \sigma_{nc}^2=\frac{1}{HW}\sum_{h,w}(x_{nchw}-\mu_{nc})^2.μnc​=HW1​h,w∑​xnchw​,σnc2​=HW1​h,w∑​(xnchw​−μnc​)2.

IN suppresses channel-wise image contrast/style statistics, useful in style transfer and some generation tasks, but can remove useful instance intensity.

Group normalization

Divide CCC channels into GGG groups and aggregate within one sample over group channels and space:

μng=1(C/G)HW∑c∈g,h,wxnchw.\mu_{ng}=\frac{1}{(C/G)HW} \sum_{c\in g,h,w}x_{nchw}.μng​=(C/G)HW1​c∈g,h,w∑​xnchw​.

Variance and affine transforms follow the shared formula. Batch independence suits small-batch detection, segmentation, and diffusion. G=1G=1G=1 resembles LN over all channels/spatial positions; G=CG=CG=C resembles IN. Exact equivalence depends on axes and affine parameter shapes.

Validate the group count

Choose a group count or channels per group that divides CCC. Fewer groups approach global LN; more approach IN. The best value depends on task and width.

Three easily confused concepts

L2 normalization maps embeddings to the unit sphere:

x^=x∥x∥2+ε.\hat{x}=\frac{x}{\lVert x\rVert_2+\varepsilon}.x^=∥x∥2​+εx​.

It neither centers nor uses batch statistics; in retrieval it makes dot products equal cosine similarity.

Weight normalization reparameterizes weights rather than activations:

w=gv∥v∥2.w=g\frac{v}{\lVert v\rVert_2}.w=g∥v∥2​v​.

It separates direction vvv from scale ggg without input statistics.

Adaptive LayerNorm uses conditions such as time or text to generate affine parameters after LN:

y=(1+s(c))⊙LN⁡(x)+b(c).y=\left(1+s(c)\right)\odot\operatorname{LN}(x)+b(c).y=(1+s(c))⊙LN(x)+b(c).

AdaLN changes where affine parameters come from, not necessarily the statistical axes; implementations may also gate residuals.

Pre-norm and post-norm in residual paths

For sublayer FFF and input xlx_lxl​, pre-norm is:

xl+1=xl+F(Norm⁡(xl)).x_{l+1}=x_l+F(\operatorname{Norm}(x_l)).xl+1​=xl​+F(Norm(xl​)).

Post-norm is:

xl+1=Norm⁡(xl+F(xl)).x_{l+1}=\operatorname{Norm}\left(x_l+F(x_l)\right).xl+1​=Norm(xl​+F(xl​)).

Pre-norm preserves a more direct identity path and often eases deep optimization. Post-norm normalizes after residual addition and can be more sensitive to initialization, warmup, and residual scale. LN/RMSNorm specify the operation; pre/post specify placement. Sandwich, final, and QK norms require architecture-specific analysis.

Microbatches, parallelism, and deployment details

Batch size and accumulation

Accumulation does not share BN statistics across microbatches. For tiny batches, consider SyncBN, freezing pretrained running statistics, validated retraining/fine-tuning with GN, or more representative sampling.

Distributed training

Ordinary BN uses local estimates; SyncBN aggregates them. LN/RMSNorm/GN/IN avoid cross-sample statistics, but tensor parallelism splitting a normalized axis can still require cross-device reductions. Batch independence is not zero communication.

Mixed precision

Variance can suffer cancellation and overflow. Stable algorithms and FP32 accumulation help. Excessively large ε\varepsilonε changes low-variance feature scaling, so larger is not always better.

Padding and masks

Token-local LN/RMSNorm keep padding out of other tokens' statistics, but attention and loss still need masks. Custom time/spatial norms must explicitly address padded elements.

Deployment and fusion

Fixed BN statistics permit static folding. Input-dependent LN/RMSNorm cannot be folded in the same way, though fused norm/residual kernels reduce memory traffic.

Freezing statistics versus parameters

Using fixed running estimates and freezing γ,β\gamma,\betaγ,β are separate operations. Specify whether one or both are frozen.

Normalization is not a complete stability design

Residuals, initialization, learning rates, optimizers, activations, sequence length, mixed precision, and anomalous data can still destabilize training.
