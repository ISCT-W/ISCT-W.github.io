Stable Diffusion: From Added Noise to Conditional Generation in Latent Space

Stable Diffusion starts from noise, repeatedly predicts how to move toward a plausible image representation, and decodes the final latent into pixels.

WJunhao WeiAbout 8 min read

Stable Diffusion starts from noise, repeatedly predicts how to move toward a plausible image representation, and decodes the final latent into pixels. Three ideas explain the process: diffusion defines noising and learned denoising; latent diffusion moves most computation into compressed representations; conditioning and guidance control how generation responds to a prompt.

This article focuses on classic Stable Diffusion v1, with an autoencoder that downsamples by eight, a U-Net denoiser, and a CLIP text encoder. Later models in the family can use different backbones, encoders, and objectives. Official repository

Forward diffusion: turning images into a simple distribution

For a real image x0x_0x0​ and noise schedule β1,…,βT\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_Tβ1​,…,βT​, a DDPM forward step is:

q(xt∣xt−1)=N(1−βt xt−1,βtI).q(x_t\mid x_{t-1}) =\mathcal N\left(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\beta_t I\right).q(xt​∣xt−1​)=N(1−βt​​xt−1​,βt​I).

q(xt∣x0)=N(αˉtx0,(1−αˉt)I),xt=αˉtx0+1−αˉt ϵ,ϵ∼N(0,I).q(x_t\mid x_0) =\mathcal N\left(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I\right), \qquad x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+ \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon, \quad\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I).q(xt​∣x0​)=N(αˉt​​x0​,(1−αˉt​)I),xt​=αˉt​​x0​+1−αˉt​​ϵ,ϵ∼N(0,I).

Training therefore samples a timestep and Gaussian noise without running all preceding steps. As αˉt\bar\alpha_tαˉt​ decreases, the signal contribution shrinks; the terminal schedule usually brings the distribution close to a readily sampled Gaussian. DDPM

At αˉt=0.81\bar\alpha_t=0.81αˉt​=0.81, the signal coefficient is 0.90.90.9 and the noise coefficient about 0.4360.4360.436. Their squares determine variance contributions. The schedule sets the signal-to-noise ratio:

SNR⁡(t)=αˉt1−αˉt.\operatorname{SNR}(t)=\frac{\bar\alpha_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}.SNR(t)=1−αˉt​αˉt​​.

High-SNR denoising primarily repairs details, while low-SNR denoising relies more on learned priors and conditions to recover coarse structure. This is an intuition, not a fixed timetable for when semantics appear along every trajectory.

Why predicting noise enables generation

A common objective trains ϵθ(xt,t)\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)ϵθ​(xt​,t) to predict added noise:

Lsimple=Ex0,t,ϵ[∥ϵ−ϵθ(xt,t)∥22].\mathcal L_{\mathrm{simple}} =\mathbb E_{x_0,t,\epsilon} \left[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|_2^2\right].Lsimple​=Ex0​,t,ϵ​[∥ϵ−ϵθ​(xt​,t)∥22​].

The timestep matters because the same pixel pattern has different meanings at different noise levels. The network learns conditional denoising over many examples; it does not possess an original image corresponding to each new generation.

The prediction yields an estimate of the clean image:

x^0=xt−1−αˉt ϵθ(xt,t)αˉt.\hat x_0= \frac{x_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)} {\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}}.x^0​=αˉt​​xt​−1−αˉt​​ϵθ​(xt​,t)​.

A common DDPM reverse-mean parameterization is:

μθ(xt,t)=1αt(xt−βt1−αˉtϵθ(xt,t)).\mu_\theta(x_t,t) =\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \left(x_t- \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}} \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\right).μθ​(xt​,t)=αt​​1​(xt​−1−αˉt​​βt​​ϵθ​(xt​,t)).

Sampling begins at xT∼N(0,I)x_T\sim\mathcal N(0,I)xT​∼N(0,I) and produces successive xt−1x_{t-1}xt−1​ values from the reverse distribution. The sampler determines random terms, variance, and the final step. “Subtract the predicted noise” is therefore incomplete.

The score interpretation connects prediction to the gradient of the noisy distribution:

sθ(xt,t)≈∇xtlog⁡pt(xt)=−ϵθ(xt,t)1−αˉt.s_\theta(x_t,t) \approx\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t) =-\frac{\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}.sθ​(xt​,t)≈∇xt​​logpt​(xt​)=−1−αˉt​​ϵθ​(xt​,t)​.

This relationship refers to the ideal predictor. Under MSE, it predicts the conditional expectation of noise given xtx_txt​, recovering the marginal score; it need not identify each particular random draw exactly. Noise prediction, x0x_0x0​ prediction, and other parameterizations can be converted, but loss weighting, numerical behavior, and scheduler configuration must agree.

What latent diffusion makes cheaper

Latent diffusion first trains an autoencoder. Encoder E\mathcal EE compresses images into z0z_0z0​, diffusion operates there, and decoder D\mathcal DD reconstructs pixels:

z0=E(x0),x^0=D(z0).z_0=\mathcal E(x_0), \qquad \hat x_0=\mathcal D(z_0).z0​=E(x0​),x^0​=D(z0​).

Compression trades perceptual fidelity against computation. Excessive compression discards detail; weak compression leaves denoising expensive. Diffusion cannot guarantee recovery of information already lost. The original work trains perceptual compression separately from generative modeling. Latent Diffusion

The upper path is training: real images provide clean latents and known-noise supervision. The lower path is inference: repeatedly denoise a random latent and decode it. Text-to-image generation does not first encode a target image. A chosen timestep sequence ends the loop; the sampler updates state and the U-Net predicts.

In v1, a 512×512512\times512512×512 image can map to a 64×64×464\times64\times464×64×4 latent. There are 64 times fewer spatial positions, but this does not imply exactly 64 times faster execution: latent channels differ from internal network channels, and the autoencoder has its own cost. The main saving is running repeated denoiser calls on a smaller grid.

The conditional latent-space objective is:

zt=αˉtz0+1−αˉtϵ,LLDM=Ez0,c,t,ϵ[∥ϵ−ϵθ(zt,t,c)∥22].z_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}z_0+ \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon, \qquad \mathcal L_{\mathrm{LDM}} =\mathbb E_{z_0,c,t,\epsilon} \left[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,c)\|_2^2\right].zt​=αˉt​​z0​+1−αˉt​​ϵ,LLDM​=Ez0​,c,t,ϵ​[∥ϵ−ϵθ​(zt​,t,c)∥22​].

The autoencoder supplies latents and the diffusion network learns their conditional distributions across noise levels. Implementations also scale latents, so encoding, noising, and decoding must use consistent scales.

How text enters the U-Net

The text encoder turns the prompt into token features ccc. Cross-attention takes queries from image-latent features and keys/values from text:

Q=WQh,K=WKc,V=WVc,Q=W_Qh,\qquad K=W_Kc,\qquad V=W_Vc,Q=WQ​h,K=WK​c,V=WV​c, CrossAttn⁡(h,c)=softmax⁡(QK⊤dk)V.\operatorname{CrossAttn}(h,c) =\operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V.CrossAttn(h,c)=softmax(dk​​QK⊤​)V.

Different spatial features can read different text information. This does not bind each word to a fixed pixel region or guarantee correct counts, relations, or spelling. The prompt participates through text encoding; CLIP's image encoder is not required in the classic v1 text-to-image path.

Classifier-free guidance and conditioning strength

Dropping conditions during some training examples teaches one network both conditional and unconditional prediction. At inference, combine the outputs:

ϵcfg=ϵθ(zt,t,∅)+w[ϵθ(zt,t,c)−ϵθ(zt,t,∅)].\epsilon_{\mathrm{cfg}} =\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,\varnothing) +w\left[\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,c) -\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,\varnothing)\right].ϵcfg​=ϵθ​(zt​,t,∅)+w[ϵθ​(zt​,t,c)−ϵθ​(zt​,t,∅)].

With this convention, w=0w=0w=0 is unconditional, w=1w=1w=1 is ordinary conditional prediction, and w>1w>1w>1 amplifies the conditional direction. Some papers write (1+w)ϵc−wϵu(1+w)\epsilon_c-w\epsilon_u(1+w)ϵc​−wϵu​, so check conventions before comparing numbers. Classifier-Free Guidance

Stronger guidance often improves prompt adherence but can reduce diversity, oversaturate images, or amplify biases. Common negative-prompt implementations replace the unconditional branch's text condition, defining a different contrastive direction. They are not logical guarantees that an object cannot appear. Evaluate adherence, quality, and multiple seeds together.

Samplers determine the path; schedules determine noise scales

A sampler updates the current latent toward the next noise level. A scheduler may also select timesteps, noise scales, and prediction parameterization. DDPM uses stochastic reverse transitions; DDIM constructs a process compatible with the same training objective, allowing deterministic sampling and fewer steps under suitable settings. DDIM

With DDIM's stochasticity parameter at zero and initial noise and settings fixed, the path can be deterministic. Different initial noise still produces different images. Fewer steps reduce network calls but increase approximation error; quality depends on the model, solver, and guidance.

Compare samplers with the same model, prompt, resolution, guidance, and evaluation. Distinguish nominal steps from actual network evaluations: higher-order solvers may call the network multiple times per step.

Image-to-image, local editing, and low-rank adaptation

Image-to-image generation encodes an input, adds noise to a chosen level, and denoises conditionally. Less noise preserves more input information; more noise permits larger changes. Local editing needs masks or other spatial conditions supported by the model and sampler. A prompt alone cannot guarantee pixel-level preservation elsewhere.

LoRA introduces low-rank updates to selected weights:

W=W0+ΔW,ΔW=αrBA,W=W_0+\Delta W, \qquad \Delta W=\frac{\alpha}{r}BA,W=W0​+ΔW,ΔW=rα​BA,

W0∈Rdout×dinW_0\in\mathbb R^{d_{out}\times d_{in}}W0​∈Rdout​×din​ remains frozen; B∈Rdout×rB\in\mathbb R^{d_{out}\times r}B∈Rdout​×r and A∈Rr×dinA\in\mathbb R^{r\times d_{in}}A∈Rr×din​ are trained. Trainable parameters fall from doutdind_{out}d_{in}dout​din​ to roughly r(dout+din)r(d_{out}+d_{in})r(dout​+din​). Diffusion adapters may target U-Net attention projections and, depending on the method, the text encoder. Target modules determine which paths can change. LoRA, Diffusers training guide

Low-rank updates reduce training state and adapter size, but do not guarantee generalization from little data. Limited poses or backgrounds can become entangled with the learned concept. Vary composition, background, prompt combinations, and seeds during evaluation, while checking retained capabilities.

Quality needs several kinds of evidence

Realism, condition matching, structural correctness, and diversity are separate dimensions. FID compares feature statistics across generated and reference sets but cannot determine whether each image satisfies its prompt. Image–text similarity can miss counts, negation, and local text. Combine structured prompts, human inspection, and task-specific metrics.

For editing, check unintended changes outside the target region. For concept adaptation, distinguish reproduction of training images from generalization to unseen scenes. One successful example cannot establish overall performance.
