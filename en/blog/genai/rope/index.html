RoPE: Encoding Position Through Rotation in Attention

RoPE treats each pair of query/key channels as a two-dimensional vector and rotates it by position.

WJunhao WeiAbout 9 min read

RoPE treats each pair of query/key channels as a two-dimensional vector and rotates it by position. For a query at mmm and key at nnn:

(Rmq)⊤(Rnk)=q⊤Rn−mk(R_m q)^\top(R_n k)=q^\top R_{n-m}k(Rm​q)⊤(Rn​k)=q⊤Rn−m​k

Absolute positions determine the individual rotations, while their attention dot product depends on relative displacement n−mn-mn−m. Standard RoPE needs no learned absolute-position table and can calculate positions beyond training length. Calculability is not reliable extrapolation: longer contexts often require frequency scaling, interpolation, and continued training.

The core mechanism

Let the rotated dimension per attention head be even, drd_rdr​. In plane (2i,2i+1)(2i,2i+1)(2i,2i+1), use frequency θi\theta_iθi​:

R(mθi)=[cos⁡mθi−sin⁡mθisin⁡mθicos⁡mθi]R(m\theta_i)= \begin{bmatrix} \cos m\theta_i & -\sin m\theta_i\\ \sin m\theta_i & \cos m\theta_i \end{bmatrix}R(mθi​)=[cosmθi​sinmθi​​−sinmθi​cosmθi​​]

A common frequency schedule is:

θi=base⁡−2i/dr,i=0,…,dr2−1.\theta_i=\operatorname{base}^{-2i/d_r}, \qquad i=0,\ldots,\frac{d_r}{2}-1.θi​=base−2i/dr​,i=0,…,2dr​​−1.

The classic base is 10000, though models may change it, rotate only part of a head, or scale frequencies. High frequencies resolve local changes; lower frequencies have longer wavelengths. Together they represent several positional scales.

Why encode position?

Bidirectional self-attention without position mechanisms is permutation-equivariant: permuting input tokens permutes outputs without explicitly representing order or distance.

Attention⁡(Q,K,V)=softmax⁡(QK⊤dh)V.\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \operatorname{softmax} \left( \frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_h}} \right)V.Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(dh​​QK⊤​)V.

A decoder's causal mask breaks full symmetry by restricting visible predecessors, but does not explicitly encode exact distances or absolute positions. RoPE adds position to Q/K matching. Sinusoidal encodings commonly use:

xm′=xm+PE⁡(m),x_m'=x_m+\operatorname{PE}(m),xm′​=xm​+PE(m),

RoPE instead projects first and rotates:

qm′=Rmqm,km′=Rmkm.q_m'=R_mq_m, \qquad k_m'=R_mk_m.qm′​=Rm​qm​,km′​=Rm​km​.

From sinusoidal encoding to RoPE

Sinusoidal encoding makes a fixed displacement expressible as a linear transformation. For one sine/cosine pair:

θi=10000−2i/d,pm(i)=[sin⁡(mθi)cos⁡(mθi)].\theta_i=10000^{-2i/d}, \qquad p_m^{(i)}= \begin{bmatrix} \sin(m\theta_i)\\ \cos(m\theta_i) \end{bmatrix}.θi​=10000−2i/d,pm(i)​=[sin(mθi​)cos(mθi​)​].

Angle-addition identities give:

pm+Δ(i)=[sin⁡((m+Δ)θi)cos⁡((m+Δ)θi)]=[cos⁡(Δθi)sin⁡(Δθi)−sin⁡(Δθi)cos⁡(Δθi)]⏟Ai(Δ)[sin⁡(mθi)cos⁡(mθi)].\begin{aligned} p_{m+\Delta}^{(i)} &= \begin{bmatrix} \sin((m+\Delta)\theta_i)\\ \cos((m+\Delta)\theta_i) \end{bmatrix}\\ &= \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\Delta\theta_i) & \sin(\Delta\theta_i)\\ -\sin(\Delta\theta_i) & \cos(\Delta\theta_i) \end{bmatrix}}_{A_i(\Delta)} \begin{bmatrix} \sin(m\theta_i)\\ \cos(m\theta_i) \end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}pm+Δ(i)​​=[sin((m+Δ)θi​)cos((m+Δ)θi​)​]=Ai​(Δ)[cos(Δθi​)−sin(Δθi​)​sin(Δθi​)cos(Δθi​)​]​​[sin(mθi​)cos(mθi​)​].​

Ai(Δ)A_i(\Delta)Ai​(Δ) is a rotation; signs depend on sine/cosine ordering. Moving by Δ\DeltaΔ rotates each frequency plane by Δθi\Delta\theta_iΔθi​.

The original Transformer adds this vector to content:

xm′=xm+pm.x_m'=x_m+p_m.xm′​=xm​+pm​.

Subsequent projections mix content, position, and cross terms, leaving the model to learn how to use them. RoPE applies the rotation directly to Q/K:

qm′=Rmqm,kn′=Rnkn.q_m'=R_mq_m, \qquad k_n'=R_nk_n.qm′​=Rm​qm​,kn′​=Rn​kn​.

Relative position enters attention geometry directly:

(qm′)⊤kn′=qm⊤Rm⊤Rnkn=qm⊤Rn−mkn.(q_m')^\top k_n' =q_m^\top R_m^\top R_nk_n =q_m^\top R_{n-m}k_n.(qm′​)⊤kn′​=qm⊤​Rm⊤​Rn​kn​=qm⊤​Rn−m​kn​.

Sinusoidal encoding adds multi-frequency phases to embeddings. RoPE applies their rotation relation to Q/K so attention scores explicitly incorporate relative displacement.

Why only relative position remains in the dot product

The rotated vectors are:

qm′=Rmqm,kn′=Rnkn.q_m'=R_mq_m, \qquad k_n'=R_nk_n.qm′​=Rm​qm​,kn′​=Rn​kn​.

Their dot product is:

(qm′)⊤kn′=(Rmqm)⊤(Rnkn)=qm⊤Rm⊤Rnkn=qm⊤R−mRnkn=qm⊤Rn−mkn.\begin{aligned} (q_m')^\top k_n' &=(R_mq_m)^\top(R_nk_n)\\ &=q_m^\top R_m^\top R_nk_n\\ &=q_m^\top R_{-m}R_nk_n\\ &=\boxed{q_m^\top R_{n-m}k_n}. \end{aligned}(qm′​)⊤kn′​​=(Rm​qm​)⊤(Rn​kn​)=qm⊤​Rm⊤​Rn​kn​=qm⊤​R−m​Rn​kn​=qm⊤​Rn−m​kn​​.​

This uses two rotation identities:

Rm⊤=R−m,RaRb=Ra+b.R_m^\top=R_{-m}, \qquad R_aR_b=R_{a+b}.Rm⊤​=R−m​,Ra​Rb​=Ra+b​.

RoPE therefore rotates with absolute positions but produces relative-position dependence in the inner product. Conventions may write m−nm-nm−n instead of n−mn-mn−m; the dependence on displacement is what matters.

A complex-number interpretation

Represent (x1,x2)(x_1,x_2)(x1​,x2​) as z=x1+ix2z=x_1+\mathrm{i}x_2z=x1​+ix2​. Rotation multiplies by a unit complex number:

zm′=zeimθ.z_m'=ze^{\mathrm{i}m\theta}.zm′​=zeimθ.

The conjugate inner product yields a phase difference:

qmeimθ‾(kneinθ)=qm‾knei(n−m)θ.\overline{q_me^{\mathrm{i}m\theta}} \left(k_ne^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}\right) = \overline{q_m}k_n e^{\mathrm{i}(n-m)\theta}.qm​eimθ​(kn​einθ)=qm​​kn​ei(n−m)θ.

Position becomes phase, and matching compares phase differences.

Standard RoPE usually rotates Q/K, not V: QK matching determines what to attend to, while V carries aggregated content. Some architectures rotate only the first drd_rdr​ channels of each head.

Two useful properties

1. Norm preservation

Rotation matrices are orthogonal:

Rm⊤Rm=I.R_m^\top R_m=I.Rm⊤​Rm​=I.

Hence:

∥Rmq∥22=q⊤Rm⊤Rmq=∥q∥22.\lVert R_mq\rVert_2^2 =q^\top R_m^\top R_mq =\lVert q\rVert_2^2.∥Rm​q∥22​=q⊤Rm⊤​Rm​q=∥q∥22​.

Larger position indices change direction and phase without directly increasing Q/K norms.

2. Same-position dot products stay unchanged

When m=nm=nm=n:

(Rmq)⊤(Rmk)=q⊤Rm⊤Rmk=q⊤k.(R_mq)^\top(R_mk) =q^\top R_m^\top R_mk =q^\top k.(Rm​q)⊤(Rm​k)=q⊤Rm⊤​Rm​k=q⊤k.

Applying the same rotation preserves content similarity. Different positions introduce a relative rotation.

Why use multiple frequencies?

The wavelength of plane iii is:

λi=2πθi.\lambda_i=\frac{2\pi}{\theta_i}.λi​=θi​2π​.

High frequencies distinguish neighboring positions; low frequencies describe longer scales. Their combination is richer than one short repeating cycle. Individual planes are periodic, but their periods differ. Positions do not all collide simply “after 2π2\pi2π.” Long-range phase combinations, finite precision, and out-of-training distances are the actual concerns.

Does RoPE guarantee distance decay?

Expanding plane contributions produces terms of the form:

∑i[aicos⁡(Δθi)+bisin⁡(Δθi)],Δ=n−m.\sum_i \left[ a_i\cos(\Delta\theta_i) +b_i\sin(\Delta\theta_i) \right], \qquad \Delta=n-m.i∑​[ai​cos(Δθi​)+bi​sin(Δθi​)],Δ=n−m.

As ∣Δ∣|\Delta|∣Δ∣ grows, dispersed phases can cancel under some statistical assumptions, suggesting locality. This is neither strict monotonic decay nor ALiBi's explicit linear penalty. Learned Q/K content can still support long-range attention.

The hard part: long-context extrapolation

For Ltarget≫LtrainL_{\mathrm{target}}\gg L_{\mathrm{train}}Ltarget​≫Ltrain​, sine/cosine remain computable, but phase combinations and distances leave the training distribution:

∣n−m∣>Ltrain.|n-m|>L_{\mathrm{train}}.∣n−m∣>Ltrain​.

Thus:

position index 可计算  ⇏  模型能可靠利用该位置\boxed{ \text{position index 可计算} \;\not\Rightarrow\; \text{模型能可靠利用该位置} }position index 可计算⇒模型能可靠利用该位置​

Problems include unfamiliar relative phases, more high-frequency wrap-around, reduced local resolution under uniform compression, and training that never learned sufficiently long dependencies.

Position interpolation

Define the extension ratio:

s=LtargetLtrain,s=\frac{L_{\mathrm{target}}}{L_{\mathrm{train}}},s=Ltrain​Ltarget​​,

Map positions by:

m′=ms,m'=\frac{m}{s},m′=sm​,

Then compute:

R(msθi).R\left(\frac{m}{s}\theta_i\right).R(sm​θi​).

This brings positions into roughly the trained range but shrinks neighboring phase differences from θi\theta_iθi​ to θi/s\theta_i/sθi​/s. Continued long-context training is usually needed.

RoPE base and NTK-aware scaling

Increasing the base lowers some frequencies and lengthens wavelengths:

θi=base⁡−2i/dr,λi=2πθi.\theta_i=\operatorname{base}^{-2i/d_r}, \qquad \lambda_i=\frac{2\pi}{\theta_i}.θi​=base−2i/dr​,λi​=θi​2π​.

A larger base is not always better: it changes local resolution and pretrained distributions. NTK-aware methods redistribute frequencies to balance short- and long-range information. Exact formulas depend on model configuration and training.

Dynamic NTK and YaRN

Dynamic NTK scales with actual sequence length. If rules change midway through a cached sequence, new queries and cached keys must retain compatible frequencies. YaRN combines frequency-dependent interpolation/extrapolation with attention-magnitude or temperature correction; follow the model's configuration and long-context training/evaluation.

Method Operation Benefit Main risk/cost
Direct extrapolation Continue positions unchanged No modification Unfamiliar phases/distances

Implementation and KV cache

A typical implementation is:

def apply_rope(q, k, cos, sin):
    q_rot = q * cos + rotate_half(q) * sin
    k_rot = k * cos + rotate_half(k) * sin
    return q_rot, k_rot

The two-dimensional rotation is:

(x1,x2)↦(x1cos⁡ϕ−x2sin⁡ϕ,  x1sin⁡ϕ+x2cos⁡ϕ).(x_1,x_2) \mapsto \left( x_1\cos\phi-x_2\sin\phi,\; x_1\sin\phi+x_2\cos\phi \right).(x1​,x2​)↦(x1​cosϕ−x2​sinϕ,x1​sinϕ+x2​cosϕ).

Interleaved layouts pair (x0,x1),(x2,x3),…(x_0,x_1),(x_2,x_3),\ldots(x0​,x1​),(x2​,x3​),…; half-split layouts pair corresponding channels from the two halves. Both express rotations, but weights, rotate_half, and sine/cosine ordering must share one convention. Switching a pretrained model's layout changes its representation.

Autoregressive decoding typically caches already-rotated keys:

{R0k0, R1k1, …, Rtkt}.\left\{ R_0k_0,\, R_1k_1,\, \ldots,\, R_tk_t \right\}.{R0​k0​,R1​k1​,…,Rt​kt​}.

Rotate only new Q/K with the correct cache position, then attend to cached keys. Check left-padding position_ids, continuity across chunked prefill/decode, position resets and cross-sample masks in packed sequences, scaling compatibility with cached keys, GQA/MQA head frequencies, and sine/cosine cache device/dtype/capacity.

FlashAttention is compatible: rotate before its kernel or fuse the operation. Speed depends on avoiding extra tensor materialization and memory traffic.

A numerical example in two dimensions

Take θ=π/2\theta=\pi/2θ=π/2, q=(1,0)⊤q=(1,0)^\topq=(1,0)⊤, k=(1,1)⊤k=(1,1)^\topk=(1,1)⊤, m=1m=1m=1, and n=2n=2n=2:

R1q=[01],R2k=[−1−1],(R1q)⊤(R2k)=−1.R_1q=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad R_2k=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\-1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad (R_1q)^\top(R_2k)=-1.R1​q=[01​],R2​k=[−1−1​],(R1​q)⊤(R2​k)=−1.

Using only n−m=1n-m=1n−m=1 gives the same result:

q⊤R1k=[10][−11]=−1.q^\top R_1k =\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}-1\\1\end{bmatrix} =-1.q⊤R1​k=[1​0​][−11​]=−1.

Moving both positions to m=2,n=3m=2,n=3m=2,n=3 keeps the dot product at −1-1−1. This holds content vectors fixed to isolate position effects. Actual hidden states also depend on context, so it does not imply unchanged network outputs after shifting a whole text.

Multimodal coordinates and implementation boundaries

Images have row/column coordinates; video adds time. Multi-axis RoPE assigns rotated channels to axes and forms relative phases along each. Results depend on coordinate indexing, frequency allocation, and trained resolutions/time spans. Replacing 1D indices with 2D coordinates does not guarantee arbitrary-resolution generalization.

The relative identity requires compatible rotations for Q and K. Different frequencies, layouts, or dynamic scaling can break it. Diagnose position/cache consistency before attributing long-context failures to model extrapolation limits.
