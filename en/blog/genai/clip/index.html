CLIP: Connecting Images and Language with Contrastive Learning

CLIP encodes images and text into a shared vector space and learns by distinguishing matched pairs from competing pairs.

WJunhao WeiAbout 8 min read

CLIP encodes images and text into a shared vector space and learns by distinguishing matched pairs from competing pairs. After training, an image can be compared with arbitrary text descriptions, giving retrieval and zero-shot classification the same basic operation.

Natural-language supervision makes this flexible: descriptions can express objects, actions, attributes, and scenes rather than fixed class IDs. They can also be incomplete, noisy, or biased. The objective identifies pairings among candidates; it does not directly train a decoder to generate captions word by word. Original paper

Why dual encoders suit retrieval

Let fθf_\thetafθ​ and gϕg_\phigϕ​ be the image and text encoders. Projection and L2 normalization give:

ui=fθ(Ii)∥fθ(Ii)∥2,vi=gϕ(Ti)∥gϕ(Ti)∥2.u_i=\frac{f_\theta(I_i)}{\|f_\theta(I_i)\|_2}, \qquad v_i=\frac{g_\phi(T_i)}{\|g_\phi(T_i)\|_2}.ui​=∥fθ​(Ii​)∥2​fθ​(Ii​)​,vi​=∥gϕ​(Ti​)∥2​gϕ​(Ti​)​.

The original CLIP studied modified ResNets and Vision Transformers for images and used a Transformer for text. Linear projections map both outputs into a shared space. “Dual encoder” means two independent representation paths, not identical architectures.

The dot product ui⊤vju_i^\top v_jui⊤​vj​ is cosine similarity because both vectors have unit length. Image vectors can be stored in advance; a new text query needs only one encoding before searching the vector collection. This scales more readily than jointly processing every image–text pair in a cross-modal network.

Compression limits fine-grained interaction. If “the red ball is left of the blue ball” and its reversed relation receive similar text vectors, the final dot product cannot recover a relation that the representation discarded.

A batch of NNN paired examples produces N×NN\times NN×N similarities. The diagonal contains the original pairs; other entries are competing candidates in the basic objective. Row-wise and column-wise classification both backpropagate through both encoders. The aim is to distinguish the correct match from alternatives, rather than simply increase its similarity without limit.

From similarities to InfoNCE

For temperature τ>0\tau>0τ>0, define the similarity logits:

sij=ui⊤vjτ.s_{ij}=\frac{u_i^\top v_j}{\tau}.sij​=τui⊤​vj​​.

With images as queries, the loss is:

LI→T=−1N∑i=1Nlog⁡exp⁡(sii)∑j=1Nexp⁡(sij).\mathcal L_{I\to T} =-\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log\frac{\exp(s_{ii})}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(s_{ij})}.LI→T​=−N1​i=1∑N​log∑j=1N​exp(sij​)exp(sii​)​.

With text as queries, apply the same calculation to columns:

LT→I=−1N∑i=1Nlog⁡exp⁡(sii)∑j=1Nexp⁡(sji),LCLIP=LI→T+LT→I2.\mathcal L_{T\to I} =-\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log\frac{\exp(s_{ii})}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(s_{ji})}, \qquad \mathcal L_{\mathrm{CLIP}} =\frac{\mathcal L_{I\to T}+\mathcal L_{T\to I}}2.LT→I​=−N1​i=1∑N​log∑j=1N​exp(sji​)exp(sii​)​,LCLIP​=2LI→T​+LT→I​​.

This symmetric objective uses in-batch negatives and has the classification form of InfoNCE. For one row, the logit gradient is:

∂ℓi∂sij=pij−1{i=j},pij=softmax⁡(si,:)j.\frac{\partial\ell_i}{\partial s_{ij}} =p_{ij}-\mathbf1\{i=j\}, \qquad p_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}(s_{i,:})_j.∂sij​∂ℓi​​=pij​−1{i=j},pij​=softmax(si,:​)j​.

A correct match assigned too little probability is pushed upward. Incorrect candidates receiving high probability experience stronger downward correction. Both encoders learn from this relative comparison.

Original CLIP learns a logarithmic parameter for the positive logit scale. If an implementation multiplies cosine similarity by exp⁡(a)\exp(a)exp(a), its temperature is τ=exp⁡(−a)\tau=\exp(-a)τ=exp(−a). Lower temperature sharpens Softmax, emphasizes difficult candidates, and changes gradient scale; it does not automatically improve representations. The official code shows the normalization and scaling details.

Batch size, negatives, and ambiguous matches

Larger batches offer more competitors and potentially finer distinctions, at the cost of larger similarity matrices and more feature communication. Distributed training often gathers device-local features into a larger candidate set; computation and gradient handling must match the implementation.

Pairs treated as negatives are not necessarily semantically wrong. Two cat photos may both be described as “a cat,” making their cross-pairings valid too. Penalizing these creates false negatives. Multiple-positive objectives, deduplication, and richer pairing information can help, but also change the data or objective.

Descriptions need not cover everything visible. If an image contains a dog, sofa, and window but the caption mentions only the dog, training may provide little incentive to preserve the other details. Broad language supervision does not guarantee complete object, attribute, or relation coverage.

Zero-shot classification constructs a classifier dynamically

For classes {c1,…,cK}\{c_1,\ldots,c_K\}{c1​,…,cK​}, insert each name into a template such as a photo of a {class} and encode it as vkv_kvk​. Classify image vector uuu using:

p(ck∣I,C)=exp⁡(u⊤vk/τ)∑j=1Kexp⁡(u⊤vj/τ).p(c_k\mid I,\mathcal C) =\frac{\exp(u^\top v_k/\tau)} {\sum_{j=1}^{K}\exp(u^\top v_j/\tau)}.p(ck​∣I,C)=∑j=1K​exp(u⊤vj​/τ)exp(u⊤vk​/τ)​.

The text encoder supplies classifier weights and the image encoder supplies features. Zero-shot means no classifier fitting on labeled examples from the target dataset. It does not mean the concepts were absent from pretraining or that the task contains no human knowledge.

Templates provide context: “crane” can mean a bird or a machine. Features from multiple templates can be ensembled. Record the templates and class set, since selecting whichever wording performs best on the test set can distort a zero-shot claim.

Probabilities depend on the candidate set C\mathcal CC. Removing a competitor or adding synonyms changes Softmax. A score of 0.9 is therefore not automatically a 90% real-world probability. Open-world use also needs to address unlisted classes, rejection thresholds, and distribution shifts.

The role and limits of image–text retrieval

Text-to-image retrieval searches image vectors with a text vector; image-to-text retrieval reverses the direction. Recall@K measures ranking quality, but results should also be separated by query type: objects, actions, colors, spatial relations, and scenes can behave differently.

For evidence-based systems, CLIP produces candidates that still require verification. High similarity does not prove an entity name, exact text, or copyright ownership. A request for “brand X packaging in the lower-left corner” may match general packaging appearance while missing the localized text. Region inspection, OCR, or other specialized checks are needed.

Global contrastive training does not directly output boxes or segmentation masks either. Such capabilities require additional structures or training, even when they reuse CLIP representations.

Connections to diffusion and vision–language models

CLIP text features can condition diffusion models. Classic Stable Diffusion v1 feeds token features into U-Net cross-attention. The diffusion network and image decoder generate pixels; contrastive learning itself does not define that process. Official Stable Diffusion implementation

Image–text similarity can also score or guide candidates, inheriting CLIP's weaknesses in relations, counting, and bias. When generation is optimized against the same evaluator used to report quality, independent metrics help distinguish adaptation to the scorer from broader improvement.

In a vision–language model, a visual encoder supplies features, a projector or connector converts them into a form the language model can consume, and a language decoder generates answers. CLIP pretraining is a useful starting point, while instruction following, detailed visual question answering, and dialogue still need appropriate training. Dual encoders, conditional generators, and conversational VLMs have different interfaces and objectives.

Evaluating reliable transfer

Report class sets, templates, and preprocessing for zero-shot classification; test difficult negatives and cross-domain queries for retrieval; evaluate OCR, counting, and spatial relations separately. Average accuracy on natural images does not establish performance in medical imaging, remote sensing, or other shifted domains.

Data sources and class design also affect bias. The paper and official model card discuss these limitations. Examine task-specific error patterns instead of treating similarity as a universally trustworthy semantic judgment.
